We use preposition `in`, `on` or `at` to say when something happens.

## in
We use `in` to talk about parts of the day, months, year, stations and long periods of time.

Examples:
- I go to the gym `in` the morning.
- I was born `in` January.
- She graduated `in` 2020.
- we go on vacation `in` the summer.

## on
We use `on` to talk about days of the week, full dates and specific days.

Examples:
- The meeting is `on` Monday.
- Her birthday is `on` July 5th.

## at
We use `at` to talk about precise times and holidays.

Examples:
- `At` 10 am, `at` noon, `at` 5:30, `at` night.
- `At` lunchtime, `At` dinnertime, `At` breakfast time.
- `At` Christmas.

# Do not use prepositions of time with time expressions
These words already indicate a specific moment in time, so it is not necessary to user the expressions.

Words: Next, Last, This, Every, Tomorrow, Yesterday, Today

Examples:
- I'll call you `next` week.
- I saw her `last` night.
- We're having a meeting `this` afternoon.
- They go to the park `every` Sunday.
- I'll finish the report `tomorrow`.
- She visited us `yesterday`.
